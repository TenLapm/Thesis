1. Introduction

Dynamic difficulty: Hamlet (Hunicke, 2005)

What it does: in the Half-Life engine, it estimates the player’s chance of death in the next 10 s from the damage taken. Above 40%, it quietly adds health.

Study, n = 20: deaths in the first 15 minutes fell from 6.4 to 4.0 (p = .051). What players believed barely matched what happened: the correlation was 0.3.

What I take from it: players are poor judges of whether a game is adapting. So the director announces every strategy, and a gate tests whether players notice.

How this project differs: for Hamlet, difficulty is the variable, and the change is hidden. Here, difficulty is held equal, and the change is in the shape of the wave, out in the open.
