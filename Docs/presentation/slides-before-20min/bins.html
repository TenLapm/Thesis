Why bins: rewards here jump at thresholds

Illustration of the idea, not measured data.

LinUCB (Li et al., 2010) assumes each arm’s reward is linear in the context. Sappers pay off only once a maze is long enough: a jump, which no straight line can show.

BinnedPosterior: one feature cut into 5 bins, 4 strategies, so 20 Beta(α, β) cells.

KernelEstimator for cells with little data: a Nadaraya–Watson average (Nadaraya, 1964; Watson, 1964) over the last 128 results, Gaussian with σ = 0.15, gives an estimate Q, used as the prior:

prior α = 1 + k·Q
prior β = 1 + k·(1 − Q)

Deep RL is ruled out: about 25 decisions per session cannot train one (Sutton & Barto, 2018).
