Dynamic difficulty: Hamlet (Hunicke, 2005)

Built on the Half-Life engine. It treats health and ammunition as an economy of supply and demand.

Estimates: records damage per enemy class, then uses a Gaussian model to give the chance of death over the next 300 ticks (about 10 s).

Policy tested: above a 40% chance of death, add health in 15-point steps every 100 ticks, aiming for a mean of 60.

Cost: about 69 µs per tick.

Study: n = 20, single-blindResult
Deaths in the first 15 min6.4 unadjusted, 4.0 adjusted; t = 2.09, p = .051
Noticing the adjustmentSmall correlation with actual adjustment (0.3)
EnjoymentNovices: no significant link. Experts: slight upward trend

What I take from it: players are poor judges of whether a game is adapting. So the director announces every strategy, and gate G7 tests whether players notice.

Where this differs: for Hamlet, difficulty is the variable, and the adjustment is disguised. Here, difficulty is held equal on purpose, and the change is in wave shape, out in the open.
